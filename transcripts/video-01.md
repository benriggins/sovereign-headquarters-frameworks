## The Four-Hour Wall [00:01:26]

Just a little over a year ago, I would only be able to work for about four hours before I would
leave the working place. I would take long breaks. I'd go play basketball. I would go and study
the Bible. I would do whatever I could to try to seem productive while staying out of work — and
my brain would be fried the second I tried to get back into it. It seemed like there was a
four-hour cap.

## Why "Just Rest More" Made It Worse [00:01:55]

So I started looking up advice. What do you do when you feel burnt out from work? And everybody
says: rest. Just rest more. It seemed so counterintuitive that I thought I had to try it —
all the great things in life seem counterintuitive at first.

And when I began to rest more, things got worse. Whether that looked like sleeping in or letting
myself off the hook and not getting into work, it did exactly what you'd think it would do — it
pushed everything back. I'd wake up the next day fully rested, hit four hours of work, and be
burnt out all over again. All it did was give me a cleaner slate to be burnt out on.

Well, I'm here to tell you today: I went from four hours of work in a day to my wife legitimately
having to tell me to get out of this desk, because it's family time. I'm not encouraging
overworking, but I've made it to the point where I've seen a 250% work increase, and the
productivity compounds over that — I went from four hours of work to ten hours of work in just a
couple of months. And rest was not the solution.

## The 3-Level Reward System, Introduced [00:03:37]

I'm going to introduce to you right now a <dfn>3-Level Reward System</dfn>. This is a very
crucial thing: rest versus reward. I discovered it first when I was reading *The 7 Habits of
Highly Effective People* — he goes deep into a principle called the <dfn>North Star</dfn>
principle. That's going to be the third reward. We're going to start with number three and see
how it all works backwards, from three to two to one.

## Level 3 — The North Star [00:04:29]

So what is the North Star principle? I'd like to think of it as a 25-year goal — something you
see purpose in, something bigger than yourself that you can't see yourself doing alone, or
anytime soon. But if there's one thing that would fulfill your desires more than anything else,
it'd be this one thing. The whole point of having a North Star is having one purpose you're
looking to. If you're a Christian, everything goes back to God and the Bible — practically, what
is the one 25-year thing you're working toward that would be filled with more of the Holy Spirit?
Stephen Covey calls this "beginning with the end in mind." I call it your 25-year purpose.

Your North Star has to be your compass. Everything points back to it — your job, your wife, your
kids, your to-do list. If what you look at every day isn't pointing to the one desire of your
heart being fulfilled before you die, I don't see your North Star actually coming to pass. And if
it doesn't, you're going to feel like you're going through the motions.

Here's a side note: what is the devil's best tool to use on you? **Good things.** The only things
you should ever be setting out to do, day by day, are the *right* things — and the right thing is
the one thing that points to the North Star.

Going a step deeper: how does your North Star relate to the purpose God gave you in this life? If
you don't know that purpose, everything else you do could still be meaningful, could still
produce money and happiness — but if it isn't fulfilling what God set out for you, then by the
time you die, all you'll have left behind is what you stored in other people's minds. Think about
C.S. Lewis — he made sure his ideas lived long after he died, through books and writing. David
wrote Psalm after Psalm. Solomon wrote the Proverbs. If you want to be truly effective in this
life, you need to outlive your flesh, and however you plan on doing that can be included in your
North Star.

What sets you apart from everybody else? What can you look at and say: nobody else's fire lights
up like mine does when they talk about this? That's in your North Star. Those are your desires —
and desires are essential to finding it. The core difference between a want and a desire: a want
comes from lacking something; a desire is something that will fulfill you, something you don't
lack, something you can envision and dream toward. The more time you spend alone with your Bible
and in prayer, the more God unveils your purpose to you — and it will never be just one thing he
doesn't keep building on.

## Level 1 — Micro Rewards [00:32:01]

Level one is called **micro rewards**. You have to give yourself daily micro rewards — level one
is all about today. What can I give myself today that'll help me finish this 90-minute work
block? Maybe I get to work on my book. Maybe I get to read. Maybe I get to record a YouTube video.

Here's one of my cheat codes: I play an NBA basketball simulation game — I'm a stat freak, and it
tells a story in the numbers. I hit a 90-minute work block, and I tell myself: give me 10 or 15
minutes on this game. Or let me write some of my book. Let me go on a drive. Let me spend time
with my wife and kids. 30, 60, 90 minutes — that's the rhythm.

## Why This Actually Works: Dopamine [00:35:04]

I asked ChatGPT: if I give myself micro rewards every 30, 60, or 90 minutes in a work day, does
this trick my brain into wanting to work? It said: **yes, this strategy absolutely works**,
because it hijacks your brain's natural dopamine-driven reward system. By spacing out small
treats, you turn a long, intimidating work day into a series of short, winnable games. Dopamine
is the neurotransmitter of *anticipation and motivation*, not just satisfaction — when you know a
reward is coming in 30 or 60 minutes, your brain releases dopamine in expectation of it, and that
chemical spike physically provides the energy and focus you need to finish the task at hand. Did
we just discover how to kill burnout? Not completely — but here's how it all ties together: when
your daily micro rewards feed into clear milestones, and those milestones point directly to a
North Star, you create a friction-free productivity loop. The only way burnout can still sneak
into this system is if you use the efficiency it gives you to overload your plate — forcing
yourself to work twice as hard without real rest. Your brain will eventually rebel.

## The Genesis Story: Rest Before Work [00:39:35]

Back to rest — we started this video with rest. Here's what people never told you about it: we
are already in rest. We already have it. It's really that simple.

I want to ask you a question: what did God do on day eight, after he rested? He stayed resting.

Picture this. God tells Adam: I need you to take dominion, be fruitful, and multiply. Adam says,
awesome — where do we start? And God says: well, hold on — let's just wake up. (Not that God can
sleep; this is a metaphor.) Adam wakes up and asks, where do we start, what do we do? And God
says: oh, I'm just resting. Adam says: wait, but how do we be fruitful and multiply and take
dominion? This is my first day here — what do I do? And God just says: oh, just rest. And he
keeps on resting.

It wasn't until Adam fell and sinned that the curse came — that's when work became toil, sweat,
thorns, and thistles. But Jesus restored to us the dominion, and restored the relationship with
the Father that God had to cut off from Adam, because he does not dwell with sin.

Hebrews chapter 4 talks about this: his works have been finished since the creation of the world.

That means I get to be in this place of rest right now, making this video. My physical body is

Now that we see it the right way — what do we do with this rest? Give yourself some practical
