# What God did on day eight

*Kingdom Stories · The Sovereign Atlas, Issue 01 (October 2026) · The Sovereign Atlas, from the teaching of Benjamin Riggins*

> Rest isn't the prize at the end of the work. For a believer, it's the place the work comes from, and that changes what a reward system is for.

Canonical page: https://sovereignheadquarters.com/magazine/issue-01/day-eight

## The two-minute version

- Benjamin Riggins asks what God did on day eight, after he rested, and answers that he stayed resting.
- In Genesis, every day of creation closes with evening and morning except the seventh, and the first full day humanity lived was lived inside God's rest.
- Hebrews 4 teaches that God's works have been finished since the creation of the world and that believers enter his rest, so the Christian works from acceptance, not for it.
- Identity is received through grace and responsibility is expressed through faithful action, which makes Christ, not the company, the ultimate North Star.
- Living spiritually from rest doesn't cancel the body's need for recovery: Jesus himself told his tired disciples to come away and rest a while.

Near the end of the video, after forty minutes on work and rewards, Benjamin Riggins circles back to where he started. He had been told to rest more. It hadn't worked for him. Now he says what nobody told him about rest.

> We already have it. And it's really that simple.

Then he asks a question most of us have never thought to ask.

> What did God do on day number eight after he rested? ... He stayed resting.

## The day with no evening

Go back to the beginning. On the sixth day, God made humanity in his image and gave the commission: "Be fruitful and multiply and fill the earth and subdue it, and have dominion" ([Genesis 1:28, ESV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis+1%3A28%3BGenesis+1%3A31%3BGenesis+2%3A1-3%3BGenesis+3%3A17-19&version=ESV)). Then, before any of that work began, came the seventh day: "he rested on the seventh day from all his work that he had done" (Genesis 2:2).

Read the text slowly and one detail jumps out. Each of the first six days closes the same way. The sixth ends, "And there was evening and there was morning, the sixth day" (Genesis 1:31). The seventh doesn't. There is no evening and morning for the day of God's rest. It is never closed off.

Benjamin tells it as a scene, careful to say it's a metaphor ("not that God can sleep"). God has just handed Adam the whole commission. Adam is ready. Where do we start? God says to wake up first. Adam wakes up and asks again. God says he's resting. Adam presses: this is his first day here, how is he supposed to be fruitful, multiply and take dominion? "And God just says, oh, just rest. And he just keeps on resting."

The first full day humanity ever lived was lived inside God's rest. Work wasn't the road to rest. Rest was the ground the work stood on.

## How work became toil

So where does exhausting work come from? Benjamin's answer is the fall. "It wasn't until Adam fell and he sinned that the curse came," he says, and work came "through toil and sweat and thorns and thistles."

Genesis is exact: "cursed is the ground because of you; in pain you shall eat of it all the days of your life; thorns and thistles it shall bring forth for you ... By the sweat of your face you shall eat bread" (Genesis 3:17–19, ESV).

Notice what the curse did and didn't do. It didn't invent work; the commission came before the fall. It changed the *conditions* of work. Then the turn: "Jesus, as we know, he restored to us the dominion, and he restored to us the relationship with the Father." God had to cut that relationship off from Adam, Benjamin says, "because he does not dwell with sin."

## Hebrews 4: the finished work

This is the passage he reads on camera, and the whole teaching rests on it. "Now we who have believed enter that rest," Hebrews 4:3 says, and then: "his works have been finished since the creation of the world." Verse 9 draws the conclusion: "There remains, then, a Sabbath-rest for the people of God; for anyone who enters God's rest also rests from their works, just as God did from his" ([Hebrews 4:3, 9–10, NIV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Hebrews+4%3A3%3BHebrews+4%3A9-11&version=NIV)).

> Doesn't this just paint a whole different picture?

If God's works are finished, the work he gives us isn't a scramble to complete what he left undone. In Benjamin's reading, God delegated dominion while he rested, and because we are in relationship with him, we rest with him. "God set up the environment and the playground and the foundation for me to do everything, and he teaches me how to do everything. But it's all from this place of rest."

Verse 11 adds a tension worth keeping: "Let us, therefore, make every effort to enter that rest." There is effort in the Christian life. But look at its direction. The striving is *toward* rest, not *for* it. You labor to stay where grace has already put you.

## From acceptance, not for acceptance

Two sayings of Jesus, read together, show the order.

"Come to me, all who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest" ([Matthew 11:28, ESV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew+11%3A28-30%3BJohn+15%3A4-5&version=ESV)). Then, in John: "Abide in me, and I in you. As the branch cannot bear fruit by itself, unless it abides in the vine, neither can you, unless you abide in me ... Whoever abides in me and I in him, he it is that bears much fruit, for apart from me you can do nothing" (John 15:4–5).

The order matters. You aren't trying to produce enough fruit to earn your place in the vine. You remain in the vine, and fruit follows.

That is the difference between working *for* acceptance and working *from* it. One person sits down at the desk thinking, I must succeed today so I can finally feel secure. Another sits down thinking, my worth isn't decided by today's results, so I can give this work my full attention, serve people well, and trust God with the outcome. Same desk, same hours, different foundation.

Paul states the shape of it. "For by grace you have been saved through faith. And this is not your own doing; it is the gift of God, not a result of works ... For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus for good works, which God prepared beforehand, that we should walk in them" ([Ephesians 2:8–10, ESV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Ephesians+2%3A6%3BEphesians+2%3A8-10%3BPhilippians+2%3A12-13&version=ESV)). And elsewhere: "work out your own salvation with fear and trembling, for it is God who works in you, both to will and to work for his good pleasure" (Philippians 2:12–13).

Put plainly: **identity is received through grace; responsibility is expressed through faithful action.** Benjamin says nearly the same thing about his own hands: "whatever I do, he's actually just doing it through me. But whose physical hands are doing it? I'm doing it."

## The North Star above the North Star

This is why a Christian's ultimate North Star can't be a company, a revenue number, or even a 25-year legacy. It is Christ himself. Benjamin's own North Star, the Sovereign Association, sits inside that. As he puts it, "if you're a Christian, then everything goes back to God and the Bible."

The 25-year purpose is a practical expression of calling and stewardship within a relationship that came first. When the company is the North Star, a bad quarter is a verdict on you. When Christ is, a bad quarter is information.

He makes it personal. While recording, his body is at a desk. His spirit, he says, "is seated in the heavenly places with Christ." That is Paul's language: God "raised us up with him and seated us with him in the heavenly places in Christ Jesus" (Ephesians 2:6, ESV). Hands at work, spirit at rest, both at once.

## Track the fruit, not just the output

If the work comes from rest, success has to be measured differently. Paul describes the goal as transformation: "be transformed by the renewal of your mind, that by testing you may discern what is the will of God" ([Romans 12:2, ESV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Romans+12%3A2%3B2+Corinthians+3%3A18%3BGalatians+5%3A22-23&version=ESV)). And he credits that change to God, not to our discipline: we "are being transformed into the same image from one degree of glory to another" (2 Corinthians 3:18).

So keep two scorecards. One tracks finished blocks, milestones and revenue. The other tracks "love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, self-control" (Galatians 5:22–23). A quarter where the first goes up and the second goes down is not a good quarter.

## Rest of soul, rest of body

None of this means the body stops getting tired. When the apostles came back from their mission, Jesus said, "Come away by yourselves to a desolate place and rest a while." Mark adds why: "For many were coming and going, and they had no leisure even to eat" ([Mark 6:31, ESV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark+6%3A31%3BMatthew+6%3A34&version=ESV)).

Living from rest is a spiritual reality. Recovering is a physical need. Both are true, and Jesus honored both. The rule that holds them together: **live spiritually from rest, work intentionally from purpose, and recover physically according to your needs.** Sleep, walks, time off, a long weekend with your family: none of that is a lapse in faith.

## The 90-minute assignment

Jesus also gave the instruction that makes the daily level possible: "do not be anxious about tomorrow, for tomorrow will be anxious for itself. Sufficient for the day is its own trouble" (Matthew 6:34, ESV).

That is the spiritual root of Level 1. You don't carry 25 years into the next block. You carry one assignment. The purpose can be immense while today's action stays small. And when the block is done, the reward isn't permission to be happy. It is gratitude for a good day's faithfulness.

Benjamin draws the same line. "Rest is not your problem," he says. "Because you have it. Maybe you don't see it the right way." So what do we do with this rest? "Give yourself some practical rewards to build yourself up to." The rewards don't produce the rest. The rest comes first, already given.

And he ends not with a technique but a relationship: to realize the rest you're in, to feel it, to live from it, "you have to have an intimate connection with him."

A 25-year calling. A 90-minute assignment. A lifetime lived from grace.

## Do this

Before your first work block tomorrow, spend a few minutes with God, not to earn rest but to remember you are already in it. Then do one 90-minute assignment from there, and leave tomorrow's anxieties for tomorrow.

## Questions people ask

**What does it mean to work from rest?**

Working from rest means treating rest as your starting place rather than a reward you earn after work. Benjamin Riggins grounds it in Genesis and Hebrews 4: God finished his works and rested, and those who believe enter that rest. In Christ the relationship with the Father is restored, so the work God gives is done in his presence, from acceptance rather than for it.

**What does Hebrews 4 say about rest?**

Hebrews 4 teaches that believers enter God's rest. In the NIV, verse 3 says God's works have been finished since the creation of the world, verse 9 says there remains a Sabbath-rest for the people of God, and verse 10 says anyone who enters God's rest also rests from their works, just as God did from his. The rest is presented as a reality to enter.

**If I live from rest, do I still need physical rest?**

Yes. Living from rest is a spiritual reality, not a claim that the body no longer gets tired. In Mark 6:31, Jesus told his disciples, who had no leisure even to eat, to come away to a desolate place and rest a while. A sound rule: live spiritually from rest, work intentionally from purpose, and recover physically according to your needs.
